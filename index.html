    Common ground

    Three churches, one ancient faith

    The Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox churches, and the Oriental Orthodox churches all trace their bishops back to the apostles — and the Catholic Church recognizes their sacraments as valid. They are not in full communion with one another, and the differences are real. But the faith they share is older than every division between them.

    Catholic Eastern Orthodox Oriental Orthodox
    1. 1

      One Creed

      All three profess the Nicene Creed — one God, Father Almighty; Jesus Christ, “God from God, Light from Light, true God from true God, begotten, not made, consubstantial with the Father”; the Holy Spirit, “who proceeds from the Father.” The baptismal faith of the ancient Church, unchanged at its core since 381.

      Where they differ: the West added “and the Son” () to the third article; the East never accepted the addition.

    2. 2

      Seven sacraments

      Baptism, Confirmation (Chrismation), the Eucharist, Confession, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony — all three celebrate the same seven mysteries instituted by Christ, and the Catholic Church recognizes Orthodox sacraments as valid.

      Where they differ: details vary — the East chrismates infants at baptism; the West confirms years later.

    3. 3

      Bishops in succession

      Each is led by bishops ordained in an unbroken line from the apostles, offering true sacrifice and true sacraments. This is why the Catholic Church calls them true particular Churches, not mere denominations.

      Where they differ: none accepts the pope’s universal jurisdiction. That claim is Catholic alone.

    4. 4

      The real presence

      All three believe the Eucharist is truly the Body and Blood of Christ — not a symbol, not a memorial only.

      Where they differ: the West explains it as ; the East leaves it a holy mystery. And they do not commune together — the wound is real.

    5. 5

      Mary and the saints

      All three call Mary — God-bearer — as defined at Ephesus in 431, honor her above all creatures, ask the saints’ intercession, and venerate icons.

      Where they differ: the Immaculate Conception and the Assumption as defined dogmas are Catholic; the Orthodox honor Mary’s holiness and her Dormition without defining them the same way.

    6. 6

      The councils

      Nicaea (325), Constantinople (381), Ephesus (431) — the councils that gave us the Creed and defended Christ’s divinity and Mary’s title — belong to all three.

      Where they differ: Chalcedon (451) parted East from Orient — the Oriental Orthodox reject its formula — and the later councils part Catholic from Orthodox.

    This shared faith is why the remaining differences deserve your hardest thinking — above all the papacy. See defense #52.
